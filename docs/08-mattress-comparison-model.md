# Mattress and Bedding: What Can Be Compared Quantitatively

Status: discussion document, 2026-09-29. Applies if mattresses are chosen as a category or a brand.
Items marked [verify] should be confirmed against manufacturer pages or directories before use.

## 1. The core problem

The two variables that most determine feel and lifespan are usually not published:
- Foam density (pounds per cubic foot) for memory foam and polyfoam layers. Predicts durability and
  sag. A few brands publish it (Tempur-Pedic, some Saatva and Brooklyn Bedding lines) [verify]; most do not.
- ILD or IFD (indentation load deflection), the engineering firmness of each layer. Rarely published.
Comfort itself is subjective and body-dependent. This is why Sleep Foundation, NapLab, and the Pillar4
sites (Sleepopolis, Mattress Nerd, Mattress Clarity) built test labs, and why Consumer Reports runs a
roller durability rig. A one-person site cannot replicate lab measurement.

## 2. What is published and comparable (Tier A, objective, from manufacturer pages and law labels)

| Attribute | Unit | Availability | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total height | inches | Always | Fit, sheet depth |
| Layer count, materials, and thickness per layer | inches | Usually (spec diagrams) | Construction normalization |
| Coil count, gauge, height, zoning | count, gauge, inches | Often for hybrids and innersprings | Support comparison |
| Latex type (Dunlop or Talalay) and blend | text | Usually for latex beds | Material class |
| Mattress weight | pounds | Usually | Handling, density proxy |
| Weight capacity | pounds | Often | Suitability |
| Brand firmness rating | 1 to 10 | Always, but self-reported and inconsistent | Normalize against independent testers' ratings |
| Cover material and fire barrier | text | Law label; fiberglass disclosure increasingly required | Fiberglass flag is a real buyer concern |
| Price by size, list versus sale | dollars | Always | Price per inch, discount depth, price history |
| Trial length | nights | Always | 100 to 365 |
| Return fee or conditions | dollars, text | Always | Net trial cost |
| Warranty length and sag depth threshold | years, inches | Always in warranty text | The single most quantifiable durability promise: a 0.75 inch threshold is stronger than 1.5 inches |
| Shipping and setup | boxed, white glove, in-home setup | Always | Convenience |
| Country of manufacture | text | Usually | Trust; FTC Made-in-USA enforcement history |
| Certifications | CertiPUR-US, GOTS, GOLS, OEKO-TEX, GREENGUARD Gold, eco-INSTITUT | Public directories | Material safety class |

Derived metrics we can compute from Tier A alone: price per inch of height; effective price after typical
discount; warranty years per dollar; net cost of a failed trial (return fee plus any shipping); density
disclosed or not disclosed (a disclosure score in itself); fiberglass present or absent; coil density per
square foot for hybrids; comfort-layer thickness ratio.

## 3. What can be cited from labs (Tier C, third-party, score plus link only)

Independent testers publish numeric results for motion transfer, edge support sinkage, pressure relief,
bounce, heat retention, response time, and durability. These are copyrighted; we may cite a score and link,
never copy tables. Consumer Reports results cannot be used commercially at all.

## 4. What we can own (Tier A proprietary, the moat)

1. Owner panel surveys. Mattress ownership incidence is near universal, so a Prolific screener is cheap.
   Ask: brand and model, purchase year, sag or body impression yes or no and depth estimate, sleep-hot
   complaint, off-gassing duration, back pain change, edge support satisfaction, motion transfer
   satisfaction, return attempted and outcome, warranty claim attempted and outcome, would buy again.
   Three hundred responses across the top 20 models gives durability and satisfaction data by model and
   by year of ownership that no lab produces, because labs test new units.
2. Site reviews with photo verification, incentive-neutral, disclosed. Same fields, structured.
3. Price history. Mattress "sales" are perpetual and discount depth is the industry's open secret. A
   daily price tracker per model and size, with list price, sale price, and days-on-sale percentage,
   exposes fake discounts. This is a genuine consumer value and is not well covered.
4. Warranty and return terms database. Sag threshold, prorated years, exclusions, return fee, and trial
   conditions parsed into fields for every model. Nobody presents this as a comparable table.
5. Fire-barrier and fiberglass database. Which models use fiberglass, which disclose, which states ban
   it. High search interest, thin coverage.
6. Complaint frequency by criterion from all evidence sources, with counts.

## 5. Rubric sketch (v1.0)

| Criterion | Weight | Type | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Durability and sag resistance | 20 | Mixed | Warranty sag threshold, disclosed density, owner survey sag rate by year, complaint frequency |
| Support and pressure relief | 15 | Subjective | Cited lab scores, owner survey by sleeper position and weight band |
| Temperature | 10 | Subjective | Owner survey sleep-hot rate, cited lab heat results, construction (coils, gel, latex) |
| Motion isolation | 10 | Subjective | Cited lab scores, owner survey |
| Edge support | 10 | Subjective | Cited lab sinkage, owner survey |
| Materials and safety | 10 | Objective | Certifications, fiberglass flag, density disclosure |
| Trial, return, warranty terms | 10 | Objective | Nights, fees, sag threshold, prorating |
| Value | 15 | Objective | Effective price per inch and per warranty year, discount honesty from price history |

Evidence floors: subjective criteria need at least 15 survey responses or 8 cited sources for the model,
or they show unscored with a confidence flag.

## 6. Honest assessment

Strengths: largest commission pool of any niche researched (about $212,000 per month across the three head
terms), flat bounties of $75 to $150 at DreamCloud and Purple, 90-day cookies at Saatva and Helix, and
several ownable data angles (price history, warranty terms, fiberglass, owner durability surveys).

Weaknesses: the deepest lab-tested incumbents of any niche, four of them under one owner; 100 to 365
night trials with commissions held or reversed on return; return rates above 20 percent; health claims
under FTC scrutiny; no public spec standard, so the data moat must be built rather than downloaded; and
the March 2026 update hit exactly this kind of listicle-driven category.

Fit with the pipeline: the pipeline works unchanged. Identity resolution is easier than appliances (few
variants per model). Spec collection is manufacturer-only. Evidence is survey-heavy. The category is
viable as a second brand once the survey and price-history machinery exists, and weak as a first category
because none of the public-domain data sources that de-risk the appliance launch exist here.
